<h2>1. What is an Attached Order?</h2><p>An attached order is an advanced trading instruction that allows investors to automatically execute trades when specific conditions are met. This type of instruction helps investors better manage their trading strategies, reduce emotional interference, and improve trading efficiency. To avoid missing opportunities during rapid price movements, attach orders can be set in advance.</p><h2>2. How to place an Attached Order?</h2><p>Locate a dropdown above the chart, select 'Manage Attached Order' to pre-set your attached orders.</p><figure class="image"><img src="https://pub.pbkrs.com/uploads/2025/14a3f6ad581fbbbf3de21efe24806b2a"/></figure><h2>3. Attached Order Settings</h2><p>Attach orders support the following three settings: price, spread, and ticks.</p><h4>(1) Price Setting</h4><p>Price setting refers to triggering trades based on specific price levels. This is the most common type of attached order setting, allowing investors to execute trades at preset price points.</p><p>Suppose the attached order is set with a price, a take-profit price of 105, and a stop-loss price of 95.</p><p>When the stock price is $100, clicking the buy order and including the attached order will generate three orders simultaneously:</p><ul><li><strong>A buy order at $100</strong>, which will immediately execute at the current market price of 100 yuan.</li><li><strong>A take-profit sell order at $105</strong>, which will automatically trigger a sell when the stock price reaches $105 to lock in profits.</li><li><strong>A stop-loss sell order at $95</strong>, which will automatically trigger a sell when the stock price drops to $95 to prevent further losses.</li></ul><h4>(2) Spread Setting</h4><p>Spread setting refers to triggering trades based on the difference between the bid and ask prices. The spread is a key indicator of market liquidity, with smaller spreads typically indicating higher liquidity.</p><p>Suppose the attached order is set with a spread, with both the take-profit and stop-loss spread set at $10.</p><p>When the stock price is $100, clicking the buy order and including the attached order will generate three orders simultaneously:</p><ul><li><strong>A buy order at $100</strong>, which will immediately execute at the current market price of $100.</li><li><strong>A take-profit sell order at $110</strong>, take-profit trigger price = 100 + 10 = 110.</li><li><strong>A stop-loss sell order at $90</strong>, stop-loss trigger price = 100 - 10 = 90.</li></ul><h4>(3) Ticks Setting</h4><p>Ticks setting refers to triggering trades based on the smallest price movement unit in the market. For example, if the minimum price movement unit for a stock is $0.01, each price change will be at least $0.01.</p><p>Suppose the attached order is set with ticks, with both the take-profit and stop-loss set at 50 ticks.</p><p>When the stock price is $100, clicking the buy order and including the attached order will generate three orders simultaneously:</p><ul><li><strong>A buy order at $100</strong>, which will immediately execute at the current market price of $100.</li><li><strong>A take-profit sell order at $105</strong>, take-profit trigger price = 100 + 0.01 * 50 = 105.</li><li><strong>A stop-loss sell order at $95</strong>, stop-loss trigger price = 100 - 0.01 * 50 = 95.</li></ul><h4>(4) Summary</h4><p>The three types of settings for attached orders (ticks, spread, and price) provide investors with flexible trading tools. These settings enable automatic execution of preset trading strategies based on market dynamics, helping investors better seize market opportunities and manage trading risks.</p><h2>4. Attached Order Operations</h2><p>(1) After setting the take-profit and stop-loss prices, you can directly check the attached orders that need to be set when placing an order.</p><figure class="image"><img src="https://assets.lbctrl.com/uploads/1_3622_micvqkw0deaiph4h7y_.jpeg"/></figure><p>(2) After placing the order, you can directly view the take-profit and stop-loss orders associated with the current order on the chart and support direct drag-and-drop modifications.</p><figure class="image"><img src="https://assets.lbctrl.com/uploads/1_3622_micvqsz45dd17yrl8bj_.jpeg"/></figure>
