<p>Hong Kong launched the Uncertificated Securities Market (USM) regime on 16 November 2026. This FAQ covers the new arrangements, including whether your physical share certificates are still valid, the fees and steps for depositing physical certificates, and whether shares held through Longbridge are affected.</p><h2>Q1: What is the Uncertificated Securities Market (USM), and when did it start?</h2><p>Hong Kong launched the Uncertificated Securities Market (USM) regime on 16 November 2026. Investors can hold and transfer participating securities electronically in their own names, without physical share certificates. This lets investors enjoy shareholder rights directly and makes the related processes simpler and more efficient.</p><h2>Q2: Which securities are covered by USM? What are “participating securities”?</h2><p>USM applies to “prescribed securities” listed on the Hong Kong Stock Exchange, including shares, depositary receipts, stapled securities, listed real estate investment trusts (REITs), equity warrants and rights. Products that cannot be withdrawn from CCASS, such as callable bull/bear contracts (CBBCs), derivative warrants and ETFs, are generally not covered.</p><p>Once an issuer completes the conversion, its securities become “participating securities”.</p><ul><li>The first list of participating securities is expected to be published on HKEX’s USM webpage on 19 November 2026.</li><li>Existing listed issuers must become participating securities in phases within five years of the USM launch.</li><li>Securities in new listings (IPOs) are issued in uncertificated form from the listing date. No physical certificates are issued.</li></ul><h2>Q3: Are my physical share certificates still valid? Do I have to dematerialise them?</h2><p>Yes, your physical share certificates remain valid. Even if your securities become participating securities, you can keep holding them in paper form. There is currently no requirement to dematerialise them (that is, convert them into uncertificated form).</p><p>Please note:</p><ul><li>Once securities become participating securities, no new physical certificates will be issued. Any new holdings can only be held in uncertificated form.</li><li>Lost or damaged physical certificates will not be replaced. They must be dealt with through dematerialisation instead.</li><li>Once dematerialised, securities generally cannot be converted back into physical certificates.</li></ul><h2>Q4: What fees apply if I deposit physical share certificates into my Longbridge account to trade?</h2><p>To support the USM launch, HKEX has revised the related fees. From 16 November 2026, third-party fees for depositing physical certificates of Hong Kong stocks and ETFs are charged according to the HKEX circular:</p><figure class="table"><table><thead><tr><th>Fee</th><th>Rate</th><th>Applies to</th></tr></thead><tbody><tr><td>Stamp duty on instrument of transfer</td><td>HK$5 per certificate</td><td>All deposited securities</td></tr><tr><td>Transfer and registration fee</td><td>0.02% of market value (based on the previous day’s closing price), minimum HK$20 per transaction</td><td>All deposited securities</td></tr><tr><td>Transactional dematerialisation fee</td><td>HK$5 per certificate, minimum HK$20 per transaction</td><td>Participating securities only</td></tr></tbody></table></figure><ul><li>If you deposit 11 or more certificates on the same day, an extra HK$5 is charged per certificate.</li><li>Actual charges are shown on your statement.</li></ul><h2>Q5: How do I deposit physical share certificates?</h2><p><strong>Step 1: Submit details online</strong><br/>Take a photo of the front of each physical certificate and send it to your account manager or Longbridge app online customer service, saying that you want to deposit shares. We will check whether the certificates can be deposited and usually reply within 2 business days.</p><p><strong>Step 2: Sign the documents</strong><br/>Sign in the transferor section on the back of each original certificate. If you use an instrument of transfer, sign that too. Your signature must match the share registrar’s records. Corporate accounts must affix the company chop and be signed by an authorised signatory.</p><p><strong>Step 3: Deliver in person</strong><br/>Bring the signed original certificates (and instruments of transfer, if any) in person to the Longbridge Hong Kong office: 18/F, One International Finance Centre, 1 Harbour View Street, Central, Hong Kong. Documents sent by post are not accepted.</p><p><strong>Step 4: Processing time</strong><br/>If all details and documents are complete, the deposit is usually completed within about 12 business days after we receive the certificates. The actual time depends on CCASS processing.</p><blockquote><p><strong>Note:</strong> The holder name on the certificates must match your Longbridge account name. For large deposits, you must also provide recent transaction records (for example, statements for the past six months) and purchase receipts.</p></blockquote><h2>Q6: Are shares I hold through Longbridge (in CCASS) affected?</h2><p>No. You can keep trading as usual.</p><p>If you later withdraw participating securities from CCASS into your own name, they can only be held in uncertificated form in your own name. No physical certificates will be issued. HKSCC charges a withdrawal fee for this.</p><h2>Q7: What is a USI (Uncertificated Securities Investor) facility? Do I need one?</h2><p>A USI facility is an electronic facility that investors open with an approved share registrar. You use it to view and manage uncertificated securities held in your own name. You can also use it to receive issuer communications, transfer securities electronically and submit some corporate action instructions (such as rights issues).</p><ul><li><strong>Clients who hold shares through Longbridge do not need one.</strong></li><li>You need one if you hold securities in your own name and plan to dematerialise them, transfer uncertificated securities or exercise certain corporate action rights.</li><li>For individual investors, the set-up fee is capped at HK$50 per facility.</li></ul><p>To open a USI facility, apply to the share registrar of the shares you hold. The main registrars are currently Computershare and Tricor.
