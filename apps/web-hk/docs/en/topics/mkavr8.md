<p style="margin-left: 0cm">Here you'll find quick answers to common questions about changing the US stock settlement cycle to T+1.</p><p style="margin-left: 0cm"><strong>1. What is the T+1 settlement cycle of US stocks?</strong></p><p style="margin-left: 0cm">The transaction date (T) refers to the date on which a securities transaction is executed. The settlement date is the date on which the assets involved in the transaction settle. According to the new US stock T+1 settlement cycle regulations, most securities transactions will settle on the next trading day (T+1) after the transaction date (T).</p><p style="margin-left: 0cm">Example: If you make a transaction on Monday, it will settle on Tuesday (assuming Tuesday is not a stock market holiday).</p><p style="margin-left: 0cm"><a href="https://www.sec.gov/oiea/investor-alerts-and-bulletins/new-t1-settlement-cycle-what-investors-need-know-investor"><span style="color: #304ffe"><u>For details, please see the SEC bulletin</u></span></a><span style="color: #304ffe"><u>.</u></span></p><p style="margin-left: 0cm"><strong>2. When did the T+1 settlement cycle take effect?</strong></p><p style="margin-left: 0cm">The T+1 settlement cycle for US stocks took effect on May 28, 2024, Eastern Time.</p><p style="margin-left: 0cm"><strong>3. Which types of assets are affected by the T+1 settlement cycle of US stocks?</strong></p><p style="margin-left: 0cm">The settlement cycle of products such as stocks, bonds, exchange-traded funds (ETFs), mutual funds, municipal securities, and real estate investment trusts (REITs) traded on US exchanges (if applicable) changed from T+2 to T+1. Click<span style="color: #304ffe"> </span><a href="https://www.dtcc.com/-/media/Files/PDFs/T2/T1-Product-List-Jan-2024.pdf"><span style="color: #304ffe"><u>here</u></span></a><span style="color: #304ffe"> </span>to see applicable types of assets.</p><p style="margin-left: 0cm">Note: US stock options were already on a T+1 settlement cycle.</p><p style="margin-left: 0cm"><strong>4. How does T+1 settlement affect my trading?</strong></p><p style="margin-left: 0cm">There is no impact on trading. You can still perform intraday (T+0) trading in US stocks.</p><p style="margin-left: 0cm"><strong>5. How is the interest of margin financing and short selling calculated after the switch to T+1?</strong></p><p style="margin-left: 0cm">Interest on arrears resulting from margin financing and short selling will begin accruing from T+1 (previously T+2).</p><p style="margin-left: 0cm"><strong>6. Are there any changes in the withdrawal rules after the switch to T+1?</strong></p><p style="margin-left: 0cm">Due to the shortened settlement cycle from T+2 to T+1, funds from the sale of US stocks may be available for withdrawal as early as T+1. The withdrawal rules for Hong Kong stock-related assets remain unchanged, with the earliest withdrawal on T+2.</p><p style="margin-left: 0cm"><strong>7. Are the auto-subscription and redemption rules for Cash Plus affected?</strong></p><p style="margin-left: 0cm">The switch to T+1 only shortens the asset settlement time after a US stock trade. The auto-subscription and redemption mechanism for Cash Plus remains unchanged.</p><p style="margin-left: 0cm"><strong>8. After US stocks switch to T+1, Hong Kong stocks still settle on T+2. How is account buying power calculated?</strong></p><p style="margin-left: 0cm">The calculation of buying power remains unchanged.</p><p style="margin-left: 0cm"> </p><p style="margin-left: 0cm"><strong>Disclosures</strong></p><p style="margin-left: 0cm"><i>This article is for reference only and does not constitute any investment advice.</i></p>
