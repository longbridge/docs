<h2>1. Long Collar </h2><ul><li>Overview</li></ul><p>Long collar is an options strategy where you hold a long position in the underlying asset, buy a put, and sell a call. This strategy helps limit losses if the asset’s price falls, while also capping potential gains if it rises.</p><ul><li>Features</li></ul><figure class="image"><img src="https://pub.pbkrs.com/uploads/2025/18dc33acd9f64a1319183ce74329d57e"/></figure><ul><li>Components</li></ul><figure class="image"><img src="https://pub.pbkrs.com/uploads/2025/49ea5230c89f656becc4dfe803b69b20"/></figure><ul><li>Profit source<ul><li>The appreciation of holding the underlying stock, while hedging the impact of the underlying stock's decline through &#34;buying a Put.&#34; </li><li>The premium income obtained from &#34;selling a Call&#34; can offset part of the cost of buying the Put, thereby achieving the purpose of hedging risks at a lower cost.<br/> </li></ul></li><li>Case study</li></ul><p>Let's imagine a made-up company called TECH.</p><p>TECH’s current stock price is $50. You are bullish on the stock in the long term but concerned about a potential short-term decline. You decide to use a Long Collar strategy.</p><p>You buy or already own 100 shares of TECH stock. Short Collar</h2><ul><li>Overview</li></ul><p>Short collar is an options strategy where you short the underlying asset, buy a call, and sell a put. This strategy helps limit losses if the asset’s price rises, while also capping potential gains if it falls.</p><ul><li>Features</li></ul><figure class="image"><img src="https://pub.pbkrs.com/uploads/2025/97b5d450831340fb737f9fe009bfc5f8"/></figure><ul><li>Components</li></ul><figure class="image"><img src="https://pub.pbkrs.com/uploads/2025/48fe54c2fc26a1af0182d1572570fdd8"/></figure><ul><li>Profit source<ul><li>The profit comes from the appreciation of holding a short position, while hedging the impact of the short position's rise through “buying a Call.” </li><li>The premium income obtained from “selling a Put” can offset part of the cost of buying the Call, thereby achieving the purpose of hedging risks at a lower cost.</li></ul></li><li>Case study</li></ul><p>Let's imagine a made-up company called TECH.</p><p>Right now, TECH's stock price is $50 per share.
