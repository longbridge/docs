<h2><strong>Options Basics</strong></h2><h3><strong>What are stock options？</strong></h3><p>A stock option is a standardized contract with a stock as the underlying asset, thus it can be traded on an exchange and settled by a clearing house.</p><ul><li>Stock options can be divided into call options and put options according to the differences of rights between right owners;</li><li>American options and European options according to the exercise method;</li><li>Cash settlement and physical delivery according to the settlement method. Most U.S. stocks and HK stocks are American options based on physical delivery, while index options are generally European options based on cash settlement.</li></ul><p>In terms of stock options, the buyer is the right owner while the seller is the obligation bearer.</p><ul><li>The buyer of an American call option has the right to buy the underlying stock at the strike price on or before the expiry date of the contract, and the seller of the call option is obliged to sell the underlying stock at the strike price if the option is exercised.</li><li>The buyer of an American put option has the right to sell the underlying stock at the strike price on or before the expiry date of the contract, and the seller of the put option is obliged to buy the underlying stock at the strike price if the option is exercised.</li></ul><p>A European option can only be exercised on the contract's expiry date.</p><figure class="table"><table><colgroup><col style="width: 16.14%"/><col style="width: 41.92%"/><col style="width: 41.94%"/></colgroup><thead><tr><th> </th><th>Call Option</th><th>Put Option</th></tr></thead><tbody><tr><th rowspan="2">Buyer</th><td>Right owner</td><td>Right owner</td></tr><tr><td>Buy the underlying stock on the expiry date</td><td>Sell the underlying stock on the expiry date</td></tr><tr><th rowspan="2">Seller</th><td>Obligation bearer</td><td>Obligation bearer</td></tr><tr><td>Sell the underlying stock on the expiry date</td><td>Buy the underlying stock on the expiry date</td></tr></tbody></table></figure><h3><strong>What is the option premium?</strong></h3><p>The option premium is the trading price of an option and the only variable in a standardized contract.</p><p>For options traded on an exchange, the price is displayed on a per-share basis, and the minimum trading contract is 1. Generally, 1 contract for U.S. stock options is 100 shares. The buyer (right owner) pays the option premium to receive the right and the seller (obligation bearer) charges option premium to fulfill the obligation.</p><p><code>Option premium </code>= <code>Option price x Contract size x Option multiplier</code></p><h3><strong>What is OTM/ITM？</strong></h3><ul><li>OTM - Out the Money</li><li>ITM - In the Money</li></ul><p>Every option has a strike price, so options can be divided into OTM &amp; ITM according to the agreed strike price and the stock’s market price fluctuations. The difference between OTM and ITM is whether the option has real value at the moment.</p><blockquote><p>How to determine the call option ITM/OTM: when the stock market price &gt; the strike price, it is ITM, otherwise, it is OTM</p><p>How to determine the put option ITM/OTM: when the strike price &gt; stock price, it is ITM, otherwise, it is OTM</p></blockquote><p>An option is an ITM when it has real value.</p><p>When trading with Longbridge Securities, you can find that ITM/OTM options have distinct colors on the options chain page, and the blue part under the T-style layout is ITM options.</p><figure class="image image_resized" style="width: 29.53%"><img src="https://pub.pbkrs.com/uploads/2025/fbd4fcf7bd92a7884bd2bf8feb0b43f3"/></figure><p> </p><h2><strong>Four Basic P&amp;L Calculations for Options Trading</strong></h2><h3><strong>Long call options</strong></h3><p>It gives the right to buy the underlying stock at the agreed price when it expires.</p><p><code>P&amp;L Calculation</code></p><figure class="table"><table><colgroup><col style="width: 21.64%"/><col style="width: 78.36%"/></colgroup><thead><tr><th>Operation</th><th>P/L</th></tr></thead><tbody><tr><td>Exercise</td><td>Profit = (stock market price - exercise price - option purchase price) * Qty</td></tr><tr><td>Waive</td><td><p>Lose options premium, which is the maximum</p><p>loss</p></td></tr></tbody></table></figure><blockquote><p>For example:</p><p>Suppose a trader buys (Long) a call option (Call) at the option premium of USD 5, with a total payout of USD 500</p><p> </p><p><strong>Two elements:</strong></p><p>Strike Price = 50</p><p>Contract Size = 100</p><p> </p><p><strong>The following situations will occur:</strong></p><p>1. Generally, 1 contract unit equals 100 shares (corporate actions may result in 1 option not being equal to 100 shares).</p><blockquote><p>For example, in the case of options contract <strong>SPY 220304 C 429000</strong>, its options premium is USD 6.68. Therefore, a total of $ 6.68 * 100 = $ 668 needs to be paid for holding this option. Upon exercise, 100 shares of SPY will be received.</p></blockquote><h3><strong>What are the trading hours for options?</strong></h3><p>The trading hours for U.S. stock options are 9:30～16:00 EST</p><blockquote><p>Daylight saving time (March - November). The above time corresponds to 21:30 - 04:00 (GMT+8)</p><p>Standard time (November - next March). The above time corresponds to 22:30-05:00 (GMT+8)</p><p>Note: Most U.S. stock options do not support pre&amp;post-market trading, but some ETF and ETN options can be extended until 16:15 EST.</p></blockquote><h3><strong>Why is the margin requirement higher for close-to-expiry options?</strong></h3><p>A close-to-expiry option refers to an option that will expire within the trading day, and if the option becomes ITM or close to the execution price at 11:00-14:00 EST, margin requirements will raise.</p><p>An option may become an ITM and be exercised on the expiry date. If a user does not have enough funds and underlying stocks required for exercise, their account may become “dangerous” and generate a margin call. Therefore, if the margin rises and a margin call occurs, users can liquidate their options position, transfer to other positions, or deposit to eliminate the margin call.</p><h2><strong>Exercise &amp; Settlement</strong></h2><h3><strong>Exercise Instructions</strong></h3><p>If a client still holds an options position after the option expires, Longbridge will “exercise the option” or “waive the exercise rights” on the user’s behalf. At the same time, the client does not be able to voluntarily exercise the option or voluntarily waive the exercise of rights.</p><h3><strong>Exercise</strong></h3><p>ITM options are exercised before the market opens on the next trading day.</p><ul><li>Call options: If strike price &lt; underlying stocks’ settlement price, then they are ITM options.</li><li>Put options: If strike price &gt; underlying stocks’ settlement price, then they are ITM options.</li></ul><h3>After exercising the option:</h3><ul><li>Long Call / Short Put: When the option is exercised, you have to buy the underlying stock at the strike price specified in the option contract. The quantity of the underlying stock you buy is determined by multiplying the option's exercise unit by 100. At the same time, the option will be expired.</li><li>Long Put / Short Call: When the option is exercised, you have to sell the underlying stock at the strike price specified in the option contract. The quantity of the underlying stock you sell is determined by multiplying the option's exercise unit by 100. At the same time, the option will be expired. If you do not hold a sufficient number of shares of the underlying stock, it may result in a short selling position.</li></ul><h3><strong>Waive</strong></h3><p>OTM options will be waived before the market opens on the next trading day.</p><ul><li>Call options: If strike price &gt; underlying stocks’ settlement price, then they are OTM options.</li><li>Put options: If strike price &lt; underlying stocks’ settlement price, then they are OTM options.</li></ul><h4>After waving the exercise rights:</h4><p>The user’s position will be null and void for either call options or put options.</p><h3><strong>Settlement</strong></h3><p>Options are settled on the T+1 day, and their underlying stocks are settled on the T+1 day.</p><h2><strong>Corporate Actions &amp; Special Situations</strong></h2><p>In the case of corporate actions and other special situations, the user's current positions will be transferred to a new position, where the option value will not change, but the underlying stock corresponding to the option may change.</p><blockquote><p>For example, 1 unit of BABA option corresponds to 100 shares of BABA underlying stock. If BABA issues a reverse stock split, 1 new share for 5 old shares, then the equity of each option equals 20 shares of BABA after consolidation, and the entire equity value does not change.</p></blockquote><p>New positions, as a result of corporate actions, can be closed but not opened.</p>
